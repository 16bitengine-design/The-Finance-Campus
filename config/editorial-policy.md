# Editorial policy: urgency that leads to hope and action

## Our purpose

The Finance Campus makes financial education compelling enough to move viewers from awareness to informed action. Our videos should sell hope: not the fantasy of effortless riches, but the belief that knowledge and consistent decisions can improve a person's financial future.

## Difficult realities are valid subjects

We may speak directly and honestly about:
- Poverty and the difficulty of escaping it.
- Debt, high borrowing costs, and debt traps.
- Inflation and the erosion of purchasing power.
- Unemployment, unstable income, and cash-flow pressure.
- Financial insecurity, emergencies, and lack of savings.
- Scams, fraud, and avoidable financial losses.
- The consequences of delaying important financial decisions.

Do not dilute a serious subject merely to keep the tone comfortable. Financial urgency is appropriate when it reflects real risks and consequences.

## Use escalation deliberately

A strong story can progressively raise the stakes:
1. Begin with a relatable situation or difficult question.
2. Show how the problem develops or compounds over time.
3. Explain the real financial consequences using sourced facts or clearly labelled hypothetical examples.
4. Reveal what the viewer can understand, change, prepare for, or do differently.
5. Give one or more realistic next steps.
6. Close with hope, agency, and encouragement to act.

Escalation should make the lesson matter. It must not manufacture panic or exaggerate the evidence.

## The emotional destination

Viewers should be able to feel:
- I recognise this problem.
- I understand why it matters.
- I do not want to ignore it.
- I have learned something useful.
- I can take a practical step.
- My financial future can improve.

## Non-negotiable safeguards

- Support factual financial claims with reputable sources.
- Distinguish facts, estimates, opinions, and hypothetical examples.
- Never present a possible outcome as inevitable or a risk as certainty.
- Never shame, ridicule, or blame people for poverty or financial hardship.
- Never exploit fear to manipulate viewers.
- Never promise overnight riches, guaranteed wealth, or guaranteed investment returns.
- Do not imply that every financial problem can be solved by individual discipline alone; acknowledge structural constraints where relevant.
- Keep advice educational and general, not personalized financial, investment, tax, or legal advice.
- End with a realistic action appropriate to the lesson.

## Editorial test

Before a script can pass editorial review, ask:
1. Is the financial problem real and clearly explained?
2. Does the escalation follow logically from evidence or a labelled example?
3. Does the viewer understand why action matters?
4. Is there a credible and realistic path forward?
5. Is there a concrete action the viewer can take?
6. Does the ending leave the viewer empowered without promising an outcome?

A script that creates fear but offers no understanding or useful action is incomplete. A script that offers hope while hiding genuine risk is also incomplete.

## Implementation status

This document and the corresponding settings define the intended editorial standard. Automated script scoring, claim-level evidence checks, and enforcement of the story arc must be implemented and tested before they are described as operational.
