# Mindset-first content framework

The Finance Campus is a financial mindset and behaviour education brand, not a financial-news or financial-data channel.

## Editorial north star

Every video should help viewers recognise a money behaviour or belief, understand its consequences, and consider a healthier response. Financial concepts are supporting tools; they are not the destination.

## Primary content pillars

1. **Money beliefs:** beliefs about wealth, poverty, success, luck, money and self-worth.
2. **Everyday habits:** spending, saving, planning, impulse buying and consistency.
3. **Financial psychology:** fear, shame, avoidance, comparison, social pressure and emotional spending.
4. **Choices and consequences:** debt decisions, lifestyle inflation, procrastination and short-term thinking.
5. **Resilience:** managing low or irregular income, setbacks, emergencies and uncertainty without shame.
6. **Long-term thinking:** patience, delayed gratification, goal-setting and the power of repeated small decisions.
7. **Financial independence:** boundaries, responsibility, ownership and building stability over time.

## Topic selection rule

Choose topics from recurring human problems and audience questions, not from the latest headlines. News feeds do not drive the topic calendar. Official educational publications may be consulted only when they help verify a durable lesson about money behaviour.

## Research rule

Use reputable sources to verify the principle being taught, check important risks, and confirm country-specific rules where they are genuinely necessary. Prefer evergreen educational resources. Statistics, current rates and detailed regulations should appear only when essential to the lesson. Never add numbers just to make a video sound authoritative.

## Video test

A topic qualifies when it can answer all four:
- What familiar money belief, habit or decision are we examining?
- Why does it matter in a person's real life?
- What simple principle helps the viewer see it differently?
- What one practical action can the viewer take?

## Creative execution

Use a relatable story, escalating but truthful consequences, a plain-language explanation, a practical step and a hopeful close. The whiteboard should illustrate the behaviour and its consequences—not display walls of financial facts. Every important spoken idea should have a meaningful visual explanation.

## What this is not

- Not a financial-news roundup.
- Not daily market or exchange-rate reporting.
- Not a statistics-heavy personal finance lecture.
- Not product promotion or personalised investment recommendations.
- Not fear-based motivation or promises of quick wealth.
